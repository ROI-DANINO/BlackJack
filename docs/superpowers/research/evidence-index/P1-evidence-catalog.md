## 2. DESIGN-ACTIONABLE FINDINGS — one line each, ready for task cards

**Layer 2 — mastery model**
- **C4 F4–F6 + C1 F27** → Ship a **deterministic, assumption-free progression** (thresholds or a moving average) rather than a probabilistic mastery estimate; Khan runs at scale with **no ability estimate at all**, and Pelánek documents an assumption-free baseline family — accepting explicitly that it estimates **no mastery quantity**.
- **C1 F28** → If an author must set item difficulty, ask **"what fraction of learners get this right?"** — absolute percentage judgement measured **r=0.80**, beating rank-ordering (.56/.62) and overturning Impara & Plake. Treat 0.80 as a **ceiling**, not a target.
- **C1 F26 + F24** → Architecture: **start every item at a published default constant, adapt per-learner online from that learner's own responses.** This is a documented, shipped architecture — and the measured cost of running with *no* predictions at all is confined to in-session accuracy (retention was null).
- **C1 F25 (ii)** → If you want the population-light estimator, **you must log response latency, not just correct/incorrect.** A correctness-only event log cannot run it.
- **C1 F8 + F21** → Do not treat "100 students" as a floor. **1PL/Rasch with Bayesian priors is calibratable near ~100; 2PL/3PL is not.** If you ever calibrate, pick the simplest model family.
- **C1 F14 + F15** → Do not surface fitted BKT-style parameters as user-visible truth: **both sides of that dispute agree fitted parameters can misrepresent a learner's knowledge state**, and neither more data nor a bigger population fixes it.
- **C1 F27 (iii)** → **Do not choose the mastery model on predictive-accuracy comparisons.** Under adaptive item selection even a trivial baseline looks accurate, while small metric differences have large application consequences.
- **C4 F13 (Table 2)** → **Do not treat "progress correlates with skill" as validation.** Minutes-spent — the metric least contaminated by prior ability — did **not** correlate (r=.132, p=.422). A mastery ladder can look validated by exactly the correlation a pure prior-ability confound would produce.
- **C4 F2** → If you copy Chess.com's puzzle rating, you inherit its seeding requirement: **new item difficulty is set by observing who solves it.** There is no shortcut in that design.

**Layer 2 — curriculum & activity design**
- **C2 F11 + C5 F4** → **Interleave hand types; do not block by category.** Two independent controlled experiments (72% vs 38%, d=1.05; 77% vs 38%, d=1.21) and, critically, **the entire benefit sits in discrimination errors (46%→10%)** — which is exactly what basic strategy is.
- **C3 F1 + synthesis** → Build **immediate, specific, corrective feedback at difficulty matched to the learner** — the one mechanism *both* sides of the deliberate-practice dispute endorse. **Never ship "hours practised" as the progress claim** — that's the contested proxy.
- **C6 F1** → Weight the curriculum toward **passive/conservative errors** (failing to hit, double, split), which are ~80% of real-table mistakes. **Direction only — no magnitude beyond the 80/20 split within this one study.**
- **C6 F3** → Ship a **calibration surface**: learners run ~62% accurate at ~80% confidence, 114/118 overconfident, and confidence–knowledge correlation is only r≈.13–.30. Show the gap explicitly.
- **C5 F2** → If you schedule, **scale the gap to the target retention horizon** (~20% of delay at weeks, ~5% at a year) rather than using a fixed interval.
- **C5 F13** → **Personalised model-based spacing beats a generic fixed schedule by 10 pp** in a real classroom — the strongest independent evidence that per-learner scheduling is worth the engineering over a one-size-fits-all schedule.
- **C5 F10** → **Active recall before reveal is separately evidence-backed** from any scheduling algorithm — build it regardless of which scheduler you pick.
- **C5 F7 + F8 + F9** → **Label SM-2, FSRS and atomicity as Product judgement / Assumption in the register, not Evidence-backed.** SM-2 is n=1 trial-and-error; FSRS's benchmark is vendor-run on vendor telemetry with a self-admitted "no fair comparison"; minimum-information is justified by analogy.
- **C5 F11** → **Do not let the learner self-grade difficulty and trust it.** No study evaluates Anki-style self-grading against ground truth, and the adjacent evidence shows subjective learning judgements invert under spacing.
- **C6 F7** → A **fluency/changing-speed criterion** with staged video instruction → rehearsal → testing is the one training protocol with a controlled demonstration in this domain. n=4; **"realistic table pace" is not supported** — pick your speeds on product reasoning.
- **C2 F9** → For a fixed-bank solo drill, an **ACT-R-derived optimal schedule** is the closest published analogue (d≈0.8–1.0) — but it still seeded item-difficulty priors from a prior population.

**Layer 3 — visual system / feel**
- **C4 F5** → If you copy a points ledger, note Khan's is **linear and undifferentiated** — no weighting by difficulty. That is a deliberate design choice to accept or reject, not a validated one.
- **C4 F6** → **Mastery must be losable.** Khan's only documented loss mechanism is a paired-question challenge with a coarse 3-outcome rule and **no passive time decay**.
- **C6 F3** → Overconfidence **changes behaviour** — bigger bets, less information-seeking. A UI that inflates confidence has a measured behavioural cost.

**Programme-level (affects how the roadmap is written, not what's built)**
- **P1-gate §6/§10** → The mastery-model choice must be entered in the **Assumption Register as a labelled Product judgement / Assumption with a named Validation Method** — not as an Evidence-backed decision (`P1-gate-summary.md:227`).
- **P1-gate §10.1** → **A verifier's correction is not self-executing.** Phase 2 needs a chartered landing pass *and* a check that corrections landed (`:317`).

## 4. THE MASTERY-MODEL QUESTION (C1) — what it establishes, and exactly where it runs out

### What C1 **settles**

1. **The structural half is clean.** Classic IRT assumes **static** ability against a fixed parameterised bank (F8, F9, F18). Elo is built to track **changing** skill and, in the education variant, does it by treating each **item as the opponent** — **no peer pool required** (F8, corroborated by Chess.com's shipped puzzle rating, C4 F1). BKT/PFA sit structurally closer to "track changing skill against a fixed item set" than either IRT or peer-Elo (F1, F2, Sufficiency statement `:132`). The charter's original framing — that Elo needs an opponent pool — **was wrong and is corrected** (`P1-gate-summary.md:201`).

2. **DKT is not worth it.** Four independent lines converge: BKT with standard extensions matches DKT (F11); Bayesian IRT matches or beats DKT and is cheaper to train (F12); DKT's advantage vanishes on ASSISTments once a 23.6% duplicate-row defect is fixed (F13); and DKT wins only on **large** datasets (F4). This is the cleanest conclusion in the dossier.

3. **The real question was reframed and is now precise: it is zero-population *item* calibration**, not model family. Of the five families examined — BKT, PFA, DKT, IRT, education-Elo — **all five calibrate item difficulty from a response population** (`:141`, `P1-gate:206`).

4. **A claimed falsification of that was tested and REJECTED.** The ACT-R rate-of-forgetting estimator (a sixth family) was argued to falsify it. The ruling: it does not, because the estimator's fixed 0.3 default is **population-derived by the source's own Methods** — "previous studies have shown to be a reasonable average across materials and learners" (`:87`).

5. **The genuinely valuable result: the requirement shrinks to a constant.** "**Population-*light*, not population-free**" (`:94`, F27 3′). SlimStampen fits a **per-learner, per-item** parameter online from that learner's own accuracy and latency, with **no population fit of that parameter and no per-item difficulty calibration** — but starting from a **single published constant (0.3)**. *"The population requirement can shrink from tens of thousands of learners to a single published constant. That is a materially different engineering problem from needing a crowd."*

6. **A measured zero-response option exists.** **Expert rating, r = 0.80** against a population-calibrated IRT reference, consuming zero learner responses (F28). Ranked: proportion correct .90 · learner feedback .88 · Elo .85 · **expert .80** · paired comparison .62/.56. From a lab fully independent of every other source on this question.

7. **A shipped alternative to probabilistic estimation exists.** Khan Academy's ladder is deterministic thresholds with **no ability estimate at all** (C4 F4–F6) — and C1 F27 supplies the academic version: **moving-average / EMA baselines classified "without assumptions"**, requiring no population-fitted parameters, which Pelánek says are preferable by default unless there's a clear reason otherwise.

8. **Direct evidence against the near-zero case.** Items attempted by ~1 learner yield **degenerate 1.0/0.0 difficulties**, causing overfitting and data leakage (F27 §5.4 / `P1-gate:218`); individualised parameters "are fitted using only few data points and thus can be significantly influenced by the noise in data."

9. **What "does the model actually measure mastery?" costs you.** The field validates BKT against **next-answer prediction**, not against mastery; "only a few studies" check the mastery estimate against a post-test (F7). And fitted parameters can misrepresent knowledge state — whether by non-identifiability (F14) or semantic degeneracy under misspecification (F15). Both agree the symptom is real.

### Where it **runs out** — the precise boundary

- **The exact configuration is unmeasured.** No source evaluates any of these on a **small, fixed, expert-authored item bank with no response population** — the product's literal shape. **Ruled SETTLED: do not collect further.**
- **No domain evidence.** Zero strategy-game, card-game or gambling-adjacent evidence across all 27 sources. Also settled.
- **The population requirement is reduced, never eliminated.** F22 needs an empirical reference to score against; F23 needs a population-calibrated anchor set upstream *and* inherits an r=0.46 ceiling from pseudo-labelling; F28's expert is asked to **simulate a reference population from memory**, and the yardstick is a government agency's calibration; F28's paired-comparison arm needs population-calibrated anchors *and* performs **worst**.
- **r = 0.80 is a ceiling, not a central estimate.** F28's 13 experts were French teachers predicting Belgian students they teach and have calibrated intuitions about. **"A blackjack trainer's author has no equivalent grounded reference population."** Plus: n=25, df=23, no CIs; the authors' own **no-generalisation disclaimer** (different domain, multi-skill items — blackjack drills are plausibly multi-skill); and **learners significantly out-performed experts, t(22)=2.71, p<.05**.
- **The assumption-free baseline path is a Q4 assertion, not a measurement.** Pelánek's "reasonable predictions" cites Pelánek 2014 and Wauters 2012 — **neither collected**; and C1-W registered *against its own interest* that the Wauters programme **does not test a moving average at all** (the strings don't appear in F28's full text). The direction survives; the attribution's precision does not.
- **The assumption-free family estimates no mastery quantity.** It discounts past answers rather than modelling knowledge — so it cannot satisfy a requirement for an interpretable mastery estimate. **That trade must be stated, not elided.**
- **Four of the load-bearing citations are one lab, one system, one commercial lineage** (F17/F24/F25/F26), one of them with a disclosed COI, with **no external replication anywhere in the dossier**. They are not four independent lines.
- **F25's reliability figures — the numbers that would say how *good* the population-light estimator is — do not exist in the dossier.** F29's sample-size analysis is likewise UNVERIFIABLE.
- **"~100 students" is an author's rule of thumb, not a measured threshold**, and F21 shows it is model-dependent (~100 for 1PL/Rasch with priors; >2,000 for 2PL/3PL or mixtures).

### The decision this leaves you

C1's own head statement — that the evidence does not settle the mastery-model choice — was **independently confirmed correct** (`P1-gate:192`). The evidence supports, *by analogy plus one measured cost figure*, this path: **start from expert-set or default difficulties → use a simple assumption-free baseline → let per-learner parameters adapt online from that single learner's own responses** (`:117`). It is **not validated for this product**. The recommendation of record: decide on product reasoning, **label it Product judgement or Assumption, and enter it in the Assumption Register with a named Validation Method** (`P1-gate:227`).

Two things you can bank without further research: **do not build DKT** (four converging lines), and **do not pick the model on predictive accuracy** (F27 §6.4 — under adaptive selection even a trivial baseline looks accurate, while small metric differences have large application consequences).

## 5. CROSS-DOSSIER CONFLICTS

1. **Is item difficulty the hard problem, or is it optional?**
 **C1 F8/F27 vs C4 F4–F6.** C1's whole load-bearing question presumes you need calibrated item difficulty — yet C1 F27 records that **basic BKT does not use item difficulty at all**, and C4 F4–F6 show a shipped, at-scale system (Khan) with **no item-difficulty parameter, no ability estimate, no probability anywhere**. Then C4 F13 shows Khan's evidence base is **weaker than assumed**. So the "just don't model difficulty" escape hatch exists and ships — but its external validation is the weakest of any option on the table.

2. **Chess.com's puzzle rating: proof of concept, or proof of the constraint?**
 **C4 F1 vs C4 F2 (and C1's charter correction).** F1 vindicates C1's structural point — education-Elo works item-as-opponent, no peer pool. F2 immediately takes it back: **new puzzle difficulty is "determined by who is able to solve it,"** and ~17 billion attempts were replayed at relaunch. The same source both confirms and defeats the shortcut.

3. **Spacing: robust, or collapsing with complexity?** *(registered as Conflict #10)*
 **C5 F1 vs C5 F3.** F1: near-universal (12/271 null). F3: **d=0.97 → 0.07** as complexity rises. Not a direct contradiction — different inclusion criteria, 20 years apart — but **citing "spacing is robust" without the complexity moderator would materially overstate what the literature supports for a decision-rule task.** **C5 F18 does not resolve it** — Latimier's non-significant stimulus-type moderator is *within verbal material*, and the review **excludes perceptual and motor learning by inclusion criterion**, so it cannot speak to F3. The conflict survives intact.

4. **Contextual interference: does mixing practice conditions help?** *(Conflict #30)*
 **C5 F16 vs C5 F17.** Two 2024 Q1 syntheses, opposite headline verdicts. **They converge where it matters most:** F16's own **applied-setting subgroup is SMD 0.23, p=0.24 — non-significant**, pointing the same way as F17's applied-domain scepticism. The disagreement is really about whether the **laboratory** effect (SMD 0.92) is the finding or the artefact. **A blackjack trainer is an applied setting.** Neither side may be cited without the other.

5. **Interleaving: strong, or weak in the applied case?**
 **C2 F11 + C5 F4 vs C5 F16/F17.** Two controlled experiments show huge interleaving gains (d=1.05, d=1.21) on exactly the situation→action structure a strategy trainer has — while the tradition that *owns* that question (CI) reports its applied-setting effect as **non-significant**. Direct tension on the design decision most likely to be near the top of the roadmap.

6. **Can a learner's starting parameter be predicted from pre-assessable individual differences?** *(Conflict #9 / register #29)*
 **C1 F16 vs C1 F26.** Positive (background covariates "substantially reduce ability estimation errors") vs **null** (WMC/GCA do not predict rate of forgetting). **Asymmetric in access, and in the reverse of the usual direction — the null is full-text, the positive is abstract-scope.** Not a clean contradiction: different parameters, different covariates, n=66 and self-described homogeneous.

7. **Does a lab-trained blackjack skill transfer to real conditions?** *(conflict register rows 17/25/26, still open)*
 **C6 F7 vs C6 F9.** F7: trained counting **generalised** to blackjack play — pro-transfer. F9: significant real-vs-lab differences in arousal and behaviour; "**doubt is cast on** laboratory gambling as a valid analogue" — anti-transfer. Neither resolves the other: F9 is about physiological authenticity, not *trained-skill* survival; F7's probes were in a **mock** casino. **The specific question — does a trained skill survive the arousal gap — is unanswered by either.** Compounded by **conflict #28**, still open: whether F8's money was won in a real or mock casino.

8. **Is the vendor/publication effect a general discount rule?** *(row #27, amended)*
 **C2 F12 vs itself, and C2 F13 vs C2 F1–F3.** The report-type moderator (peer-reviewed g=.28 vs nonjournal g=.02) is robust in the **unadjusted** analysis and **fails under both specifications in the adjusted one**, with the peer-reviewed estimate **reversing to –.04**. **Do not use it as a general discount rule.** But it is **independently corroborated at study level by a different source and method** — in WWC's five-study algebra set, both positives are **vendor-authored (Carnegie Learning)** and all three independents are indeterminate. That corroboration is **unaffected** by the Table 3 non-replication. Same pattern appears in **C5 F8** (FSRS benchmarked by FSRS) and **C4 F7/F8/F11** (Khan-partnered, Khan-staffed, Gates-commissioned).

9. **What does "chess is the best-evidenced games domain" buy a blackjack product?**
 **C3 F3 vs C3 F11.** F3's games figure (24%, the **highest** of five domains) is chess-dominated — deterministic, full-information, adversarial. F11's predictability moderator says the *structurally* apt figure is the low end (**6%**). The dossier resolves the internal contradiction by ruling: **F11 governs on whether the literature bears on low-predictability tasks (it does, via a slope); the coverage gap governs on whether any study is in blackjack's domain (none is).** So the headline 24% is the **least** applicable number on that card and the 6% is not assignable either.

10. **Does ITS beat the alternative, or just beat nothing?**
 **C2 F1/F2/F3 vs C2 F12/F13, and C4 F7/F8/F10/F11 vs C4 F13.** The identical asymmetry appears on two independent cards: **every positive result compares the intervention against less of it or none of it; the comparisons against an equal-time active alternative are null.** C2 F3: no advantage over individual human tutoring (g=–.11) or small groups (g=.05). C4 F13: no detected difference against equal-time active controls. **That asymmetry is itself the finding** — and it licenses "no benefit detectable here," **not** "no benefit" (F13 is one underpowered 4-week quasi-experiment whose outcome instrument was 21 items from a **grade-six** test given to seventh graders).

11. **Expert difficulty judgement: absolute or rank-ordered?** *(registered conflict-adjacent, lead #40 — only one side has been read)*
 **C1 F28 vs Impara & Plake (1998), uncollected.** F28 found absolute-percentage judgement (**.80**) **beat** rank-ordering (.56/.62), explicitly contradicting the standing Impara & Plake result. Directly product-relevant if you ever ask an author to set difficulty — but **only one side of this disagreement has been read.**

12. **A same-card conflict worth carrying: abstracts overstate their own papers.** *(Conflict #10, C1)*
 **C1 F22's abstract vs F22's body.** Abstract: configurations "approached the upper end of the accuracy range reported for human experts." Body: correlations "were lower than those reported for pooled human judgements (.70)… and lower than .76." Not a contradiction — different comparators — **but a dossier quoting only the abstract overstates the method, which is exactly what this dossier did until the correcting pass.**
